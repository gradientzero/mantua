## The structural limitation, which matters for anything cited from this

**It is a compilation, and it carries no dates or episode numbers for its constituent
interviews.** The seven conversations were recorded over a span of years and the edit gives no
markers between them beyond the speaker headings. So nothing quoted here can be dated, placed
in sequence, or traced back to the original episode without going to tim.blog and finding the
source interview. The only date this source establishes is the compilation's own publication,
7 August 2026.

Consequence for the wiki: the notes attribute claims to speakers, never to a moment. Anything
that would need a date — "Sanderson's current word count", "what Godin thought in year X" —
is not supported by this source, and none of it is asserted.

## Three things left unverified, and kept out of the wiki

The transcript asserts these; nothing in it establishes them, and none were carried into the
notes:

- **"Just do it" originating with Gary Gilmore.** Godin's aside — that the phrase was the last
  thing Gilmore said before his execution and that it is the origin of the Nike slogan. Ferriss
  reacts with surprise and says people can research it themselves, which is where the
  transcript leaves it. Not repeated in the wiki.
- **Asimov's 400 books and six-hour typing shift.** Godin reports both from a collaboration
  years earlier. The six hours are used in the notes because the *practice* is what the point
  rests on and it is his first-hand account; the book count is not used.
- **The *Paris Review* line's date.** Ferriss cites "The Art of Fiction, Number 72" without a
  year. The note cites it the same way rather than supplying one.

One smaller thing: Lamott's account of her husband's inner-critic exercise switches pronouns
mid-description (a client's critic and his own are both in the same sentences). The note
renders it in the second person, which is a smoothing of an ambiguity in the transcript rather
than a reading of it.
