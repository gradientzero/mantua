# Publish the writing-craft notes, or soften the link into them

**Owner action.** The 2026-08-09 ingest of the Tim Ferriss writing compilation created two
notes as `status: draft`, which is the default for ingested material:

- [`content/notes/writers-on-starting-finishing-and-bad-drafts.mdx`](../content/notes/writers-on-starting-finishing-and-bad-drafts.mdx)
  — the source note
- [`content/notes/separating-drafting-from-judging.mdx`](../content/notes/separating-drafting-from-judging.mdx)
  — the concept note that connects it to the harness material

The already-published [`generator-evaluator-loops.mdx`](../content/notes/generator-evaluator-loops.mdx)
gained a paragraph pointing at the second one, so in production that link currently renders as
a muted dashed-underline span (the draft policy in `README.md`). Same situation as
`tasks/2026-07-30-publish-dashbench-note.md`, but smaller: one link, not five, and the
paragraph stands on its own without it — it states the claim and the inversion before it
points anywhere.

Two things to decide, and they are separable:

1. **Publish the two notes.** Nothing about them is provisional; they are a summary of a
   public transcript and an analysis of it, in the same register as the rest of the notebook.
2. **Decide whether the notebook is publicly open in topic.** This is the real question and
   it is why this was not just published. `README.md` and the home page both say the notebook
   is not about a single topic and that subjects emerge from what accumulates — but every
   published page so far is agentic engineering, so publishing these two makes the claim true
   in public for the first time. That is an editorial decision, not a filing one.

If the answer to (2) is "not yet", option (1) is still available for
`separating-drafting-from-judging` alone: it is legible as an evaluation note to a reader who
has never heard of the episode, and it would leave the source note as the draft. That is the
awkward half-measure, though — it publishes the analysis and hides what it is analysing.

**Recommend publishing both**, on the grounds that the claim in the README is either true or
should be removed.
